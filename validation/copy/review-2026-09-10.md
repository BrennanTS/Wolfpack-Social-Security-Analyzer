Reviewed `validation/copy/corpus.md` (194 sentence shapes, 41 sources,
generated from 500 households) against `validation/copy/RUBRIC.md`. The
corpus was current: newer than every copy module it draws from.

## What the copy is doing well

Rule 1 is the strongest part of this corpus, and it is the rule that matters
most. The widow(er)'s limit is stated in its counter-intuitive form rather
than smoothed over — "Beta is shown receiving $2,475.00/mo after that death,
more than the $2,112.00/mo Alpha was being paid" — with the reason given in
the same breath. Where the model is incomplete the copy says so and says what
SSA would actually do: "the chart shows them nothing from that death until
their own benefit begins. SSA would pay a survivor benefit over those
months." A recovered benefit names the year its dollars are in. Rule 2 is
explicit where it needs to be ("This is not a recommendation: the
recommendation above is built with one filing date per person and cannot
model a separate survivor claim date"). Rule 5 holds throughout: absence is
stated in words, never as a blank or a zero.

Four findings. One is about the review apparatus rather than a sentence, and
it is much the most consequential.

### The corpus does not contain the client report at all

- **Source:** `validation/sweep/surfaces.ts` (the corpus generator)
- **Rule:** the process behind all five
- **Line:** n/a — this is about what is *absent*.
- **Why it matters:** `CLIENT_LAYOUT` is cover, intro, answer, changes,
  survivor, action, terms, limits, disclosure. **None of those blocks are
  modelled by `surfaces.ts`**, so none of their copy is in this corpus. What
  the corpus does cover — `HouseholdSection`, `PersonSection`,
  `WidowedSection`, `MethodologyAppendix` — is the older block set.

  Everything built in the layout work is therefore reviewed by nothing
  except `reportCopy.test.ts`'s banned-word list. No duplicate-sentence
  check, no sentinel check, no screen-versus-print agreement, and no
  per-surface jargon guard reaches the headline answer page, the action
  plan, the terms page, the limits page or the firm's disclosures. That is
  the copy a client reads first and reads most.

  It also means yesterday's per-surface jargon guard is weaker than it
  looks: it passed, but it never opened the client report.
- **Suggested:** extend `surfaces.ts` with a `reportSurface(analysis, layout)`
  that walks the same blocks `ReportDocument` walks, so the corpus and every
  sweep check see what the client is handed. Until that exists, this review
  can only speak for the adviser-facing half of the document.
- **Resolution:** done in `b236eb5`. `reportSurface` now covers cover, intro,
  answer, changes, survivor, action, assumptions, terms, limits and the
  disclosures. The corpus went from 194 sentence shapes to 284, and the
  sentinel, duplicate, screen-versus-print and jargon checks all reach the
  client report. Two blocks are still out by design and the code says why:
  `longevity` and `solvency` need sensitivities the caller computes, so a
  household alone cannot produce their sentences.

### A bare acronym in a chart caption a client reads

- **Source:** `CombinedIncomeChart.combinedIncomeCaption`
- **Rule:** 3, Understandable without a glossary
- **Line:** "...that personal band keeps growing at the assumed COLA on its
  own, exactly as it would without the survivor segment..."
- **Why it matters:** `COLA` is introduced on the terms page as
  "(Social Security calls this a cost-of-living adjustment, or COLA)" — but
  this caption sits under a chart on an earlier page, where a reader meets
  the acronym before the definition. The same sentence family already writes
  it out in full everywhere else, including the *other* branch of this very
  function ("before any cost-of-living adjustment"), so the app is
  inconsistent with itself within one caption. A reader who does not know
  the acronym stops at it, and the sentence it interrupts is the one
  explaining how to read the chart.
- **Suggested:** "...that personal band keeps growing with the yearly
  cost-of-living increase on its own, exactly as it would without the
  survivor segment..."
- **Resolution:** done in `b236eb5`, with the suggested wording. A second
  instance turned up in `widowedCopy.widowedIncomeCaption`'s nominal branch,
  which this review had not seen because the widowed surfaces only render
  real dollars; it was fixed at the same time. The test that pinned the old
  phrasing now pins the claim and asserts the acronym is absent.

### The same caveat printed twice on the widowed page

- **Source:** `pdf/WidowedSection.widowedLifetimeCaption` and
  `pdf/WidowedSection.widowedIncomeCaption`
- **Rule:** 4, House style — "sentences beside each other must not repeat"
- **Line:** the lifetime caption ends "...in today's dollars, before any
  cost-of-living adjustment." and the income caption, roughly ten lines
  below on the same page, ends "Amounts are in today's dollars, before any
  cost-of-living adjustment."
- **Why it matters:** a reader who has just been told something and is told
  it again in the same words stops trusting that the second sentence is
  telling them anything new — and on this page the second caption *is*
  carrying new information ("the two benefits are one payment, and SSA pays
  the larger"), which the repeated clause buries. `copy.sweep.ts`'s
  duplicate check cannot see this: it compares whole sentences, and these
  two differ everywhere except the clause that repeats.
- **Suggested:** drop the trailing clause from `widowedIncomeCaption`. The
  lifetime caption above already establishes the basis for the whole page,
  and it establishes it beside the figures it qualifies.
- **Resolution:** done in `b236eb5`. The default basis is now stated once per
  page. Nominal still states itself wherever it appears: it is the basis a
  reader opts into, it contradicts the page default, and it has to be
  flagged.

### The rubric contradicts the code it is meant to govern

- **Source:** `validation/copy/RUBRIC.md`, rule 3
- **Rule:** n/a — the rubric itself
- **Line:** "No term a reader would have to look up. `PIA`, `FRA`,
  `present value`, `discount rate`, `mortality-weighted`, `RIB-LIM` and
  `optimizer` appear only on the terms page..."
- **Why it matters:** `present value` was deliberately allowed onto client
  surfaces, and the terms page now introduces it under "Lifetime value".
  `copy.sweep.ts` no longer bars it. A rubric that forbids what the code
  ships teaches the next reviewer that the rubric is out of date and can be
  skimmed, which costs far more than the one line.
- **Suggested:** remove `present value` from that list and add a sentence
  saying it is permitted because the terms page defines it — so the
  reasoning survives rather than just the outcome.
- **Resolution:** done in `ec42043`. Rule 3 now permits it and records why
  barring it failed: the same words reached the printed report from three
  components while a test asserted the term was banned, because that test
  read one module and the sentences were assembled in others.

noted rather than argued.

review does not spend effort rediscovering it.

## Found after this review, by other means

Recorded here so the trail from this review's own gap is visible.

Closing finding 1 — extending the corpus to the client report — is what made
the compliance samples possible, and building those found two things no
reading of the corpus could have:

- **A widowed report carried no action plan.** The `action` block was
  `shapes: LIVING`. Its copy was written partly for a widow(er), and the one
  step with a deadline on it — the $255 lump sum, claimable within two years
  of the death — reached only households where nobody had died. Fixed in
  `b92fa25`, with the survivor claim as its own dated step and a past-tense
  death step.
- **An apply step dated in the past.** The apply month is the filing month
  less three, so a household whose best filing age is near today was told to
  have applied last October. Now "As soon as you can". Fixed in `b92fa25`.

Neither is a sentence defect, which is why a schedule of sentences could not
show them and a rendered sample could. That is the argument for sending both
to a reviewer.
