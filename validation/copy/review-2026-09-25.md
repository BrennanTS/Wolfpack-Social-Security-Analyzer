# Copy review, 2026-09-25

> **Status: closed 2026-09-25.** All 29 findings were acted on the same day;
> each carries its outcome below. Two findings in this review were partly
> wrong, and their resolutions say how: the table of changes is always in
> today's dollars, and "Both as early as possible" is a widowed label, not a
> second name for a couple's plan.

Reviewed `validation/copy/corpus.md` (295 sentence shapes, 75 sources, 500
households) against `validation/copy/RUBRIC.md`. The corpus was current: it
was regenerated this morning and is newer than every copy module it draws
from. The longevity and solvency pages are still outside the corpus by
design, so their copy was read directly from `src/components/pdf/reportCopy.ts`.

**This review had a second brief:** simplify. Make the copy shorter and easier to
understand, and give a compliance reviewer less to mark, without losing
accuracy and while staying close to SSA's own terms. Each finding below is
judged on the rubric. The section after the findings covers volume
separately.

## What the copy is doing well

The hardest rules are the best kept. The widow(er)'s limit is stated in its
counter-intuitive form and explained in the same breath ("Beta is shown
receiving $2,475.00/mo after that death, more than the $2,112.00/mo Alpha was
being paid"). Where the model is incomplete, the copy says what SSA would
actually do rather than letting the chart speak for it. The action plan gets
the operational facts right, and gets them in SSA's words: survivor benefits
cannot be applied for online, the $255 lump sum has a two-year window, and
Part B is penalized for life if you enroll late. The limits page is honest,
specific and short. The cover and intro now describe the report instead of
issuing instructions. Rule 5 mostly holds.

The problems cluster in two places. **First, correctness drifted as the
model changed underneath the words.** The report basis became switchable,
the household value stopped being mortality-weighted, and a few sentences
still describe the old behavior. **Second, the on-screen captions under the
strategy table and the income chart explain the model's internals** in the
model's own vocabulary ("increment above the personal band", "death
direction implied by each spouse's own life-expectancy input"). They are
the longest sentences in the app and the hardest to read.

29 findings: **6 high, 13 medium, 10 low.** Two findings are marked
**needs a human check**. Each could be a copy error or a calculation error,
and this review cannot tell which.

## High

### A Future-value report calls its figures "today's money" and says they are discounted

- **Source:** `report/Answer.lifetimeCaption`, `report/Changes.note`,
  `report/Terms.body` ("The yearly increase" and "Lifetime value")
- **Rule:** 1, Correct about Social Security ("a lifetime sum is not a
  present value")
- **Line:** "What you receive over your lifetimes, in today’s money" /
  "Figures in this report are in today’s money, so they already allow for
  that. A figure of $3,000 a month means $3,000 of today’s buying power" /
  "later payments are counted for less"
- **Why it matters:** When Report basis is set to Future value,
  `ReportDocument` restates every figure into undiscounted future dollars.
  These four sentences are constants, so they don't change. The headline
  number on page one then sits under a caption that describes a different
  quantity, and the glossary explains a method the report did not use. This
  is the exact defect class the rubric names first: calling a straight sum a
  present value. The corpus cannot show it, because `surfaces.ts` renders
  the report blocks only in real dollars (see *What the corpus does not
  contain*). `solvencyTableCaption` and `householdValueCaption` were already
  made basis-aware for this reason. These four were missed.
- **Suggested:** make each a function of `dollarsMode`, as those two are.
  - Caption, present: "Lifetime value of your benefits, in today’s dollars"
  - Caption, future: "Total paid over your lifetimes, in future dollars"
  - Changes note, future: "Monthly amounts once each change takes effect,
    including the assumed 2.50% yearly increase."
  - Yearly increase term, future: "Figures in this report include an assumed
    2.50% increase each year, so they show what the check will say, not
    what it will buy. (Social Security calls this a cost-of-living
    adjustment, or COLA.)"
  - Lifetime value term, future: "Every payment added up as it is paid.
    Nothing is discounted for being further away."
- **Resolution:** Done. `lifetimeCaption(mode, hasSpouse)`, `changeTableNote(mode)` and `keyTerms(mode, cola)` replace the constants, and `ReportDocument` passes the basis to the answer, changes and terms blocks. One correction to this finding: the table of changes is built from the engine's bands (`incomeChanges` reads `analysis.periods`), which the basis restatement does not touch, so its monthly amounts are in today's dollars in both bases. Its note was therefore right to say today's dollars. In future value it now says so as the exception ("Unlike the lifetime figures, they leave out the yearly increases") rather than switching to future dollars. `ReportDocument.test.tsx` renders both bases, and the new tests were confirmed to fail against the old caption.

### The methodology card caps the survivor benefit at what the deceased was receiving

- **Source:** `pdf/MethodologyAppendix.survivorBenefitCard` (`widowedCopy.ts:186`)
- **Rule:** 1, Correct about Social Security ("the widow(er)'s limit
  genuinely lets a survivor be paid more than the deceased was receiving")
- **Line:** "Where the deceased had already filed, it is capped at what he
  was receiving."
- **Why it matters:** This states the widow(er)'s limit backwards. If the
  deceased claimed early, the cap is the *larger* of what they were paid and
  82.5% of their full benefit. That is why `survivorFloorNote` elsewhere in
  the same report shows a survivor paid more than the deceased received.
  Every widowed report prints both statements. A reviewer who notices the
  conflict has to assume one of them is wrong. The test at
  `widowedCopy.test.ts:36` pins the incorrect wording.
- **Suggested:** "A survivor benefit can start at 60 and is reduced for each
  month before the survivor’s full retirement age, which follows its own
  schedule. If the deceased claimed early, it is limited to the larger of
  what he was receiving and 82.5% of his full benefit."
- **Resolution:** Done, with the suggested wording. The test that pinned "capped at what she was receiving" now pins "limited to the larger of", and asserts the old phrase is absent.

### The widow's-limit definition reads as a guarantee

- **Source:** `report/Terms.body` ("The widow’s limit")
- **Rule:** 1, Correct about Social Security
- **Line:** "If the spouse who died had claimed before their full retirement
  age, the survivor receives the greater of what that spouse was actually
  paid and 82.5% of that spouse’s full benefit."
- **Why it matters:** The limit is a ceiling, not a payment. A survivor who
  claims at 60 receives 71.5% of the deceased's full benefit, which is below
  the 82.5% this sentence says they "receive." This is on the terms page of
  four layouts, which is where a reader goes to settle what a rule means.
- **Suggested:** "If the spouse who died had claimed before their full
  retirement age, the survivor benefit is limited to the larger of what that
  spouse was paid and 82.5% of their full benefit. That is why a survivor
  can be paid more than the person who died. Claiming the survivor benefit
  early lowers it further."
- **Resolution:** Done, with the suggested wording. The heading is now "The widow(er)’s limit".

## Medium

### The survivor page credits any delay, when only the higher earner's counts

- **Source:** `report/Survivor.intro`
- **Rule:** 1, Correct about Social Security
- **Line:** "When one of you dies, the other keeps the larger of the two
  benefits, not both. Waiting raises that figure for whoever lives longer,
  for every year they live on."
- **Why it matters:** When the lower earner waits, the survivor benefit
  doesn't change. The larger benefit is the higher earner's, and only that
  person's claiming age sets it. The terms page gets this right ("The age
  the higher earner claimed at sets that figure"). The survivor page
  contradicts it, and a couple may read it as a reason for both of them to
  wait.
- **Suggested:** "When one of you dies, the survivor receives the larger of
  your two benefits, not both. The later the higher earner claims, the
  larger that benefit is, for every year the survivor lives."
- **Resolution:** Done, with the suggested wording.

### The death step states two conditional things as certain

- **Source:** `report/Action.deathStep`
- **Rule:** 1, Correct about Social Security
- **Line:** "The survivor benefit does not start on its own. A one-time
  lump-sum death payment of $255 is also due to the surviving spouse and
  must be claimed within two years."
- **Why it matters:** SSA converts a spousal benefit to a survivor benefit
  automatically once the death is reported. The $255 is payable to a spouse
  who was living with the deceased, or who is eligible on their record. It
  is not simply "due." A widowed client may be told they must act when they
  needn't, or told they are owed a payment they aren't.
- **Suggested:** "Report the death to Social Security. If the survivor
  benefit does not start on its own, apply for it. A one-time $255
  lump-sum death payment may also be payable to the surviving spouse, and
  must be claimed within two years."
- **Resolution:** Done, for both the couple's step and the widowed step.

### The note on the estimated benefit misstates when cost-of-living increases start (needs a human check)

- **Source:** `WidowedPanel.piaEstimateNote`, `pdf/WidowedSection.piaEstimateNote`
- **Rule:** 1, Correct about Social Security
- **Line:** "a check includes every cost-of-living rise since he filed and
  this figure includes none, which means $3,053 is in 2025 dollars."
- **Why it matters:** SSA applies cost-of-living increases from the year a
  person turns 62, not from when they filed, so "since he filed" is wrong
  for anyone who filed after 62. The claim that the figure "includes none"
  may also be untrue. `deceasedPia` (`deceased.ts:80-164`) solves for the
  benefit that reproduces the check amount, with no increase removed, so
  the recovered figure carries whatever increases the check did. The form
  asks only for "Monthly check he received," without saying whether that
  means the first check or the last. If advisers enter the last check, the
  survivor figures are in that check's dollars and the note names the wrong
  year. This review cannot settle it, because the answer depends on which
  check advisers enter and what the engine expects.
- **Suggested:** decide which check the field means, and label it that way
  ("Most recent monthly check"). Then say what follows: "This benefit was
  estimated from the monthly check you entered, so it includes the
  cost-of-living increases paid up to that check."
- **Resolution:** Done, on the assumption that advisers enter the most recent check. The field now reads "Last monthly check he received", its hint no longer dates the increases from the filing, and the note names the year of the death (the year the last check was paid) as the figure's basis. The calculation is unchanged. If the field should mean the first check instead, the recovery in `deceasedPia` would need to remove the increases, and the note would change back.

### Automatic Medicare enrollment is promised one to three months too early (needs a human check)

- **Source:** `report/Action.medicareAutomatic` (gated at `ReportSections.tsx:392`)
- **Rule:** 1, Correct about Social Security
- **Line:** "Medicare starts on its own, because you will already be receiving
  Social Security."
- **Why it matters:** The gate is `starts <= sixtyFive`. SSA's automatic
  enrollment applies to someone already receiving benefits at 65, and the
  usual guidance is to be receiving them about four months before. A
  client whose benefits start in the last few months before 65 is told
  enrollment is automatic when they may need to sign up. Getting this wrong
  costs a Part B penalty that lasts for life. Verify the exact SSA/CMS
  threshold before changing the gate.
- **Suggested:** widen the gate so that only a start at least four months
  before 65 gets this sentence. Everyone else gets `medicareManual`.
- **Resolution:** Done. medicare.gov confirms the rule (payments starting "at least 4 months before you turn 65"). The gate is `medicareStartsAutomatically` in `src/lib/medicare.ts`, with a boundary test. The manual step's wording now states the four-month rule.

## Where the words are (the simplification brief)

The corpus holds about **12,200 words** across its shapes. Most of the
length is concentrated in a few captions:

| Source | Longest shape | Suggested | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `survivorIncomeCaption` | 133 words | ~36–60 | advocacy line, internal terms |
| `combinedIncomeCaption` | 109 | ~38 | explains chart construction |
| `spousalMethodologyCopy` | 105 | ~50 | repeats "under the recommended strategy" in one sentence |
| `survivorClaimNote` | 88 | ~55 | typo; disclaimer runs long |
| `householdValueCaption` | 84 | ~38 | says one thing twice |
| `widowedRecommendationDetail` + lifetime caption | 66 + 63 | ~35 + ~15 | basis stated twice; stale clause |

Those six rewrites remove about 400 words from what the most common
household sees, and nothing a client needs goes with them. What goes is
the model explaining itself. Three principles would keep it from coming
back:

1. **State the dollars basis once per page.** Only the exception gets
   flagged. The report already works this way. The screen captions still
   restate the basis in every caption.
2. **Say what the client is paid, not how the chart or engine builds it.**
   Words like *segment*, *band*, *increment*, *engine*, *direction* and
   *input* belong in the methodology appendix, which is exempt from
   rule 3. They don't belong under a chart.
3. **Name a limitation once, beside the figure it affects, and stop.**
   Several notes add a second sentence explaining why the first is true.
   A compliance reviewer reads every added sentence as another claim to
   verify.

> **Resolved 2026-09-25**, except the longevity and solvency pages.
